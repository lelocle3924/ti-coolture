## 3. The navigation

One pill, every page. Shape is Direction A's floating capsule; behaviour is the
settled hide-on-scroll from UX-TASKS 1.1.

It carries its own dark material rather than sitting in a violet band, because
it has to read over three different grounds: `/products` and `/stores` run on
paper, the homepage opens on a photograph, and `/about` opens on violet.

Two consequences worth recording:

- The homepage no longer ships its own chrome. `CurrentNav` and its search
  overlay left `Homepage.tsx` for `src/components/Header.tsx`.
- A floating pill sits *over* the page instead of pushing it down, so every
  route except the homepage now clears the pill's height in `SiteShell`. The
  homepage is the exception on purpose: its hero owns that space.

### The Hidden Gems inversion

The team asked for the tab to appear exactly when the nav goes away. Verified by
measurement, not by eye:

| | header transform | gem tab transform |
|---|---|---|
| scrolling **down** | `translateY(-130px)` — gone | `translateX(0)` — out |
| scrolling **up** | `translateY(0)` — back | `translateX(48px)` — tucked |

This **reverses UX-TASKS 2.3**, which had the tab hiding *with* the chrome. The
spec line is now wrong and should be updated when someone next touches that
document.

## 4. The hero

Direction A's card deck, on the violet field, headline removed — that line is
being set into the background artwork, so setting it in type here would print it
twice.

The deck is inset rather than full-bleed, which is what gives the identity marks
somewhere to live.

### The brand surround

All three brand-device studies are gone. In their place, per today's direction:
an arc at 80% opacity on the left, the ribbon loop at 80% on the right, and a
looping line running across the page between them
(`src/components/BrandSurround.tsx`).

Three things about it that are constraints rather than preferences:

- **It does not animate.** The deck is already the one moving thing on that
  screen. A drifting background would be a second — the exact attention tax that
  was flagged against Direction A's marquee, and against the guidance that says
  animate one or two elements per view at most.
- **The run is an SVG `<pattern>` in `userSpaceOnUse`**, not a repeated element.
  The motif starts and ends on the same baseline with the same horizontal
  tangent, so it tiles seamlessly at any width — no visible joins.
- **It threads under the deck, not behind it.** Centred behind the deck it was
  covered at every width that matters. On phones the band under the deck is too
  shallow, so there it runs above instead.

## 5. The vertical overflow — the real bug

The report was "looks right on a MacBook, overflows on Windows". It was real,
it was measurable, and it was worse than reported: **it also clipped on the
1440×900 MacBook**, just by less.

Cause: sections pinned to `h-[100dvh]` with `overflow-hidden` and content sized
in `rem`. The content does not shrink when the viewport does, so the bottom row
goes under the fold with no way to reach it.

Measured, before the fix — "How it works", content height vs the box it is
pinned into:

| viewport | content | box | over by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1440×900 MacBook | 829px | 790px | 39px |
| 1600×900 | 822px | 772px | 50px |
| 1536×864 | 804px | 738px | 66px |
| 1920×1080 @125% | 815px | 760px | 55px |
| 1366×768 | 753px | 640px | **113px** |
| 1280×720 | 728px | 592px | **136px** |

The fix is to size the pinned content from `dvh` instead of `rem` — clearance,
card min-height, card padding, internal rhythm, the step counter and the
heading all take a viewport-height term. Same treatment for the reveal footer,
which was putting its legal row below the fold on a 560px viewport.

### How it was checked

A harness (`scratchpad/viewport-audit.js`) walks **17 viewport sizes × 8 pages**
and reports two classes of failure: a document wider than its viewport, and any
viewport-locked box whose own content does not fit inside it. Sizes are content-
area heights — i.e. after browser chrome — across 1920/1600/1536/1440/1366/1280
laptops at 100/125/150% scaling, iPad landscape and portrait, and five phones
from iPhone SE to Pro Max.

- Before: **46 findings**.
- After: **CLEAN — no overflow at any size.**

No horizontal overflow was found at any point, before or after.

## 6. Everything else that moved

- **What's in store** tiles are square and rounded, not landscape. Square is
  also the ratio the product photographs are actually shot at; the landscape
  crop was cutting the top and bottom off every piece.
- **Product page** images rounded, matching.
- **The map** was lifted out of the homepage into `src/home/DistrictMap.tsx` so
  `/discover` can carry the same one. It navigates nowhere itself — the parent
  passes `onOpenRoute` and `onPin`, so a pin leaves for `/discover` from the
  homepage and selects a stop in place on `/discover`.
- **Pins are individually clickable now.** They were decorative
  (`pointer-events-none`) inside one big island button; each is its own control
  and deep-links to its own stop.
- **The shop profile** got study 2's layout because a shop cover is shot
  landscape (21:9 desktop / 3:2 mobile, UX-TASKS 6.1), which is the ratio that
  layout wants. A product photograph is square and was being cropped to death
  by it.
- **The About page** has no blog listing, as asked. Its copy is not written:
  every chapter and passage is a labelled placeholder that says so on screen.
  The empty measures show the team how much room the copy has before it is
  written, rather than filling it with invented history.

## 7. Bugs found and fixed on the way

- **Nested `<button>`** — the new pins sat inside the island button. Invalid
  HTML that React refuses to hydrate. The island is a plain click target now
  and the keyboard route into a district is an explicit link below the map.
- **A truncated SVG path** — my own edit dropped six numbers from the map pin's
  `d` attribute, which the browser reported once per pin. Restored.
- **The `NAV_LINKS` type error** in the old header is gone with the file.
  `npx tsc --noEmit` is now clean; it had two standing errors this morning.
